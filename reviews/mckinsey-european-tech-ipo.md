## Structure

Title, numbered executive summary spanning five points, then five numbered sections walking through the argument in order: (1) the prosperity gap between Europe and the US, (2) the IPO capital and market-cap gap, (3) post-IPO performance comparability, (4) the quantified economic loss to Europe, (5) structural causes in the European stock market and the three challenges to address. The deck uses prefix numbering on titles (1., 2., 5.) to reinforce section location. No dedicated dividers, no appendix, no team slide. The close is a challenges-and-root-causes table rather than a recommendation or next-steps slide.

## Slide-by-slide

### Slide 1 — Title
**Title:** "Overcoming the European Tech IPO Challenge"
**Role:** Open the deck; establish McKinsey as author; date the POV.

**Issues:**
- None.

**Take:** Standard McKinsey cover; subtitle "Leveraging Europe's Innovation Capabilities" pairs problem and asset in one line, and the July 2024 date anchors the POV in time.

---

### Slide 2 — Executive summary
**Title:** "Executive Summary – European Tech IPO Opportunity"
**Role:** Compress the five-part argument into one standalone slide with a headline stat under each step.

**Issues:**
- [3] "Competetiveness" misspelled in step 5 body text
- [3] Card 1 begins "But Europe is falling behind..." without a preceding clause; the contrast is orphaned
- [3] Title is a topic label ("Executive Summary – European Tech IPO Opportunity") rather than a sentence that states the answer; reader has to walk the five points to assemble the thesis

**Take:** The five-step timeline with paired stat underneath is a genuinely good exec-summary device, but the headline does not commit to the answer the five steps collectively support.

---

### Slide 3 — Chart (prosperity gap)
**Title:** "1. Bridging the Prosperity Gap: Leveraging Europe's Innovation Capabilities"
**Role:** Establish the top-of-funnel problem: US enterprise value has outgrown Europe's 8 to 10x since 2000, and the gap is tech-driven.

**Issues:**
- [2] Title is a topic label ("Bridging the Prosperity Gap") rather than an action title stating what the data shows
- [3] Left chart label says "Economic value gap mainly driven by tech companies" but the stacked bars do not visually call out tech; the reader has to do the color-key work themselves
- [3] "EXAMPLARY" label on the right-hand card block (should be "EXEMPLARY" or "ILLUSTRATIVE")
- [3] The six innovation-field cards on the right are data points with no unifying structure; they sit as a list

**Take:** Strong scene-setter with real scale data (8x, 10x), but the lead is a section header instead of the claim the chart actually makes.

---

### Slide 4 — Stat panel (VC as driver)
**Title:** "1. Growth and VC proves to be an attractive asset class as a key driver for economic growth"
**Role:** Argue that VC funding is the mechanism behind the prosperity gap, and that Europe's VC is half the US on a GDP basis.

**Issues:**
- [2] Subject-verb disagreement in title: "Growth and VC proves" (should be "prove")
- [2] Four big-number stats (47%, 9x, 4.7, 82%) describe what VC does globally, but none is EU-vs-US; only the small right-side chart carries the comparison the title implies
- [3] "Growth catalysator" is non-standard English; "catalyst" is the term
- [3] Source says "Global fund performance by equity strategy, 2000-2020, %" but none of the four stat cards is a return or performance figure; the subtitle does not describe what is shown

**Take:** Right role in the argument (VC is the lever), but four of the five data points are about start-ups generally, not about the EU-US delta the slide is supposed to prove.

---

### Slide 5 — Chart (IPO capital)
**Title:** "2. From 2015 to 2023, IPOs in the US raised ~340 billion USD more than those in Europe"
**Role:** Quantify the aggregate IPO capital gap and show it widened after 2017.

**Issues:**
- None.

**Take:** Clean action title with a number, paired aggregate and time-series views, plus a key-findings sidebar that calls out the 2018 inflection; this is the deck's cleanest analytical slide.

---

### Slide 6 — Chart (tech market cap differential)
**Title:** "2. The total IPO value differential was particularly high in the tech sector with 11.6x"
**Role:** Narrow the capital gap to tech and show the gap is much wider there (11.6x) than across all IPOs.

**Issues:**
- [3] The tab toggles at top left ("Tech IPOs / All IPOs / Capital raised / Market cap") are a navigation device that implies this is one view from a larger dashboard; as a static PDF slide, the other tabs are inert and confusing

**Take:** Title commits to 11.6x and both charts show exactly that; the 3-year breakdown honestly discloses that the gap varies (10.7x, 15.6x, 8.7x) rather than papering it over.

---

### Slide 7 — Chart (decomposition)
**Title:** "2. Lower IPO market cap of Tech companies in Europe is driven by lower valuations and lower number of IPOs"
**Role:** Decompose the 11.6x tech gap into its two drivers: fewer IPOs (+590) and lower average size (+154%).

**Issues:**
- [3] Decomposition shows count x avg-size, but 165 x 0.9 = 149 and 755 x 2.2 = 1,661, not the 142 and 1,654 in the left bars; the arithmetic is close but not exact and no reconciliation note appears
- [3] The multiplication symbol between the two right panels is small and easy to miss; the structural claim (count times size equals total) depends on the reader seeing it

**Take:** MECE decomposition is the right move for the section and the two drivers are genuinely independent; the slide makes a clean analytical point.

---

### Slide 8 — Chart (annual series)
**Title:** "2. The US and Europe both recorded the highest number of Tech IPOs in 2021"
**Role:** Show the year-by-year series behind the 2015-2023 aggregates, and disclose that 2021 was an outlier peak for both regions.

**Issues:**
- [2] Title picks a secondary finding (2021 peak) when the slide's dominant visual is the overwhelming US-vs-EU gap every year; the title does not match the chart's actual message
- [3] The top two rows (#IPOs and avg market cap) are in count and USD bn respectively but the relationship to the bottom row (total market cap) is not visually drawn; reader has to mentally multiply

**Take:** Honest disclosure of the 2021 anomaly is intellectually good, but the title underplays what the chart primarily shows, which is structural US dominance every year.

---

### Slide 9 — Chart (post-IPO performance)
**Title:** "3. On average US performance is comparable to EU performance – perception often driven by few prominent cases"
**Role:** Pre-empt the obvious counter-argument ("but US IPOs perform better, so it is rational to list there").

**Issues:**
- [3] Em dash in title ("EU performance – perception"); minor but note the deck uses it inconsistently
- [3] Left chart (positive vs negative distribution) uses different units from the middle and right charts (annualized return by cohort); reader must swap mental models across the three panels
- [3] Callout at bottom cites four specific 2022 outliers (-87%, -88%, -89%, -97%) but does not quantify how much of the -24% cohort average they explain; the "outliers drive the average" claim is stated, not shown

**Take:** Addressing the skeptic's counter-argument explicitly is the right move and rarely done; execution is slightly compromised by mixing two chart types.

---

### Slide 10 — Waterfall (economic loss)
**Title:** "4. Europe's engagement with the US IPO market"
**Role:** Derive the $439B economic-loss figure by adding back European companies that listed in the US and subtracting US companies that listed in Europe.

**Issues:**
- [1] Title is a bare topic label ("Europe's engagement with the US IPO market") on the slide that contains the deck's single biggest number; the $439B headline claim is not stated in the title
- [2] The waterfall's construction is not self-evident: two horizontal bars (top "US", bottom "Europe") with +244 and +12 adjustments, then a right-side stacked bar showing 244 + 195 = 439; the reader cannot follow the derivation without the footnote, and the footnote reads "market cap at IO plus increase in market cap" (typo, should be "IPO")
- [3] Typo "Europen" in axis label; typo "1.675" should be "1,675" in the right-side sidebar (European thousands separator inconsistent with rest of deck)
- [3] Right sidebar frames $439B as "~26% of current DAX40 market cap"; the DAX40 is German, not European, which is a category mismatch for an EU-wide argument

**Take:** The slide is doing the deck's most load-bearing math, and the typos, topic-label title, and opaque construction make the single most important number the hardest to verify.

---

### Slide 11 — Comparison (market structure)
**Title:** "5. European stock market is more fragmented than US one and without clear tech-focused exchange"
**Role:** Name the structural cause: 35 EU listing exchanges vs 3 in the US, no tech hub equivalent to NASDAQ.

**Issues:**
- [3] "than US one" is awkward phrasing for a client deliverable; "than the US" reads more naturally
- [3] "Source: Web research" is a vague source for three specific counts (35, 41, 18); a client reviewer would ask "which web research"
- [3] Bullets on both sides are descriptive but not parallel (EU bullets name problems, US bullets name features); the comparison works but the structure is not quite MECE

**Take:** The six numbers tell the story on their own; the supporting bullets add texture without adding rigor.

---

### Slide 12 — Table (challenges)
**Title:** "5. If the European stock market were to address 3 challenges it could increase competitiveness"
**Role:** Name the three root-cause challenges and rank them in implied priority.

**Issues:**
- [2] Hedged action title ("were to address...could increase"); for a POV closing slide this should commit
- [2] No recommendation, no sequencing, no next steps; the deck ends by naming challenges rather than proposing actions, asks, or a reform agenda
- [3] Challenge 3 (regulatory) is explicitly downgraded in the Importance column ("While regulation is a factor, it is not the decisive reason"), which is honest but leaves the reader wondering why it is on the list at all
- [3] The three icons on the left (shapes, downward bag, gavel) are decorative rather than semantic; the gavel for "regulation" is the only one that obviously maps

**Take:** The slide is the deck's close and does name the three right things, but a POV that ends on "if X were to happen" reads as diagnostic rather than directive.

**Take:** Reads mostly as a story, but with two weak links. Slide 3 opens the argument with a topic-label ("Bridging the Prosperity Gap") and slide 10, the most important number, also uses a topic label. A reader scanning only titles would pick up the gap, the drivers, and the fragmentation, but would miss the $439B claim entirely. The close hedges ("could increase").

**Template saturation:**

Low. The deck uses different chart types on almost every slide: stacked bar, big-number panel, aggregate and breakdown pair, decomposition, annual time series, two-panel distribution, waterfall, side-by-side comparison, and challenges table. The "Key findings" right-hand sidebar appears on slides 5, 6, and 8 with consistent formatting, which is appropriate because those are parallel analytical views of the same question. The navigation tabs ("Tech IPOs / All IPOs / Capital raised / Market cap") on slides 6, 7, 8 imply a dashboard origin.

**Take:** This is authored, not scaffolded. Chart craft varies with the analytical question in the right way.

**Inconsistencies:**

Number formatting varies. Most of the deck uses "$340 bn" / "USD bn" style; slide 10 sidebar writes "1.675 USD bn" with a European decimal-as-thousands-separator. Title casing is mixed: slide 2 uses a dash as a divider, slides 3-12 use prefix numbers ("1.", "2.", etc.), slide 1 uses no prefix. Em dash appears in slide 9 title but nowhere else. Three spelling or grammar errors (Competetiveness slide 2, "proves" slide 4, Europen and IO slide 10) across a 12-slide deck is high for a published McKinsey POV. Source lines are consistent in format but vary widely in specificity, from "Capital IQ; Crunchbase" (named databases) to "Web research" (slide 11) and the unnamed IMF/Bureau sources on slide 3.

**Take:** Chart craft is tight; copyediting is not. The typo on the most important slide (10) is the kind of thing a senior reviewer would catch.

**Generic applicability:**

The deck passes the swap test. It is specifically about the European tech IPO ecosystem, with named metrics (35 listing exchanges, $439B loss, 11.6x gap, 2015-2023 window), named exchanges (NASDAQ, DAX40), and a specific European regulatory and structural argument. You cannot swap "European" for another region and have the content work.

**Take:** Substantively region-specific.

## Overall take

The argument architecture is professional-grade: a five-part exec summary, a section per claim, a dedicated counter-argument slide (9), and a derivation of the headline $439B loss figure (10). The biggest tell of a draft rather than a published artifact is the copyediting: three spelling or grammar errors, a typo on the most important derivation slide, and topic-label titles on the two slides that most need to commit (3 and 10). The single change that would lift this deck most is rewriting the titles on slides 3, 8, 10, and 12 to state the claim the chart supports, starting with slide 10, where "Europe's engagement with the US IPO market" should become something like "Europe lost $439B in economic value from 2015 to 2023 by listing companies in the US rather than at home."

Note on calibration: the score looks harsh for a McKinsey-branded artifact, but the severity tally reflects genuine findings against the rubric's definitions (topic-label title on a load-bearing slide = severity 1, hedged close = severity 2, typos in a client deliverable = severity 3 each). The deck's analytical content is strong; its execution reads as a working draft that would have been copyedited and retitled before external release.
