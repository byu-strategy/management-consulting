# Slide Element Taxonomy (v1)

Derived from atomizing two decks visually, slide by slide, from first principles:

- **McKinsey, European Tech IPO** (12 slides, reference deck, professional)
- **Uber: An AV Solution** (19 slides, student team deck, near-professional)

Rule I followed: an element counts as "atomic" if it is (a) visually distinct, (b) recurring across slides or across decks, (c) something a slide builder would consciously add as a unit ("I need a sidebar here"; "I need a source line"). Roles ("exec summary slide", "section divider") are *compositions* of atoms, not atoms.

Target: 10-30 atoms. Landed on **28**.

---

## The 28 atoms

### A. Frame atoms (page-level furniture)

**1. Title** — the primary slide headline. Variants: *action title* (commits to a claim, e.g., "From 2015 to 2023, IPOs in the US raised ~340 billion USD more than those in Europe"), *topic label* (bare label, e.g., "Conclusion", "Europe's engagement with the US IPO market"). Content quality (action vs topic) is a grading dimension; structurally it's the same atom.

**2. Kicker** — small label *above* the title. Forms seen:
- Section number prefix attached to the title itself ("1.", "2.", "5.") — McKinsey
- Typographic tag before the title ("EXECUTIVE SUMMARY", "THE OPPORTUNITY | 1 OF 3", "APPENDIX", "RECOMMENDATIONS & NEXT STEPS") — Lululemon, Collin Powell
- Section/phase banner ("So What?", "Key Findings") — mixed

**3. Title divider rule** — horizontal line directly under the title separating it from body. Present in the Uber student deck every analytical slide; absent in McKinsey (which lets whitespace do the work).

**4. Source line** — footer citation. Examples: "Source: Capital IQ" (McKinsey), "Source: Lululemon Form 10-K (FY2024), Item 7 (MD&A)…" (Lululemon). One line, small font, bottom-left.

**5. Footnote** — numbered clarification keyed to a superscript in the body/chart ("1. EU27, UK, Switzerland, and Norway"). Usually between source line and page number, small font.

**6. Page number** — single numeric figure, almost always bottom-right corner.

**7. Brand mark** — recurring logo, wordmark, or color stripe. Variants:
- Corner logo ("McKinsey & Company" bottom-right on every page)
- Edge stripe (vertical "Uber" wordmark running up the left edge of every body slide)
- Both (Lululemon uses a red left stripe *and* a footer "Lululemon Intelligence Brief | G. Gould")

**8. Metadata block** — title-slide-only cluster: authors/team, organization, date, confidentiality notice. Not decomposable further for grading purposes; a slide either has it or doesn't.

### B. Text blocks

**9. Paragraph** — unstructured prose block, no bullets.

**10. Bulleted list** — vertical list with hanging-indent bullets.

**11. Sidebar panel** — right-column text panel paired with a main visual on the left. Canonical examples: McKinsey "Key findings" and "Further insights" sidebars; Uber "Key Implications" sidebar; Lululemon "THE REAL STORY" and "THE MATH" red-bordered panels. Structurally a (header + bulleted list or bulleted list of short paragraphs) unit glued to a chart.

**12. Stat block** — oversized numeric figure with a smaller descriptor label, treated as a standalone content unit. Examples: "47% of newly created jobs are generated by start-ups" (McKinsey S4), "+41% China Mainland revenue" (Lululemon), the "35 / 41 / 18" vs "3 / 16 / 1" big-number columns on McKinsey S11. Differs from an annotation callout (27) because a stat block is a content primitive, not a chart overlay.

### C. Chart primitives

**13. Bar / column chart** — includes vertical and horizontal, single-series, grouped, stacked, and 100%-stacked. McKinsey S3, S5, S6, S7, S10; Uber S2, S9, S10, S13.

**14. Line / area chart** — including stacked area and cumulative. Uber S3 (stacked area), Uber S6 (line), Uber S14 (line with highlighted point).

**15. Waterfall / bridge chart** — numeric buildup or breakdown showing additive/subtractive components. McKinsey S10 (two-stage waterfall), Lululemon S3 (FY2024 revenue bridge).

**16. Scatter / bubble chart** — 2D space with bubble size as optional third dimension. Uber S15.

**17. Map** — choropleth (Uber S7) or geo-annotated.

### D. Non-chart visuals

**18. Harvey-balls matrix** — rows × columns grid where each cell is a partial-fill circle (or equivalent) encoding a qualitative score. Uber S11. Close cousin: any fixed-category visual-scoring grid.

**19. Process arrow / timeline** — sequence of numbered or labeled steps connected by arrows. Seen as:
- Horizontal numbered timeline (McKinsey S2, 5 circles on an arrow)
- Horizontal arrow-shaped header row above a table (Uber S17, three phase arrows over the roadmap)
- Vertical flag-shaped step list (Uber S5/S8/S12 section menus — arguably a subtype of atom 23)

**20. Comparison panel** — two parallel columns with identical structure (same labels, same chart types, same stat positions) to contrast two entities. McKinsey S11 (EU vs US market structure); Lululemon S2 (Americas vs International).

**21. N-card grid** — 2 to 4 equally sized cards arranged horizontally (most common) or vertically. Each card is optionally {icon, heading, body, sub-label}. Examples: Uber S4, S16 (3 horizontal cards with icons + green-highlighted body); Lululemon S1 (4 numbered cards 01/02/03/04); Collin Powell S8 (3 recommendation cards with "Output:" sub-labels). Vertical-orientation variant: Uber S19 (3 icon+paragraph rows stacked).

**22. Data table** — rectangular row/column grid with cells that can hold text, bullets, or nested elements. Header row often colored or bolded. McKinsey S12 (3 challenges × 3 columns with bulleted cells); Uber S17 (roadmap 3×3); Uber S18 (2-column risk/mitigation).

**23. Section menu** — mini-TOC used as a slide-between-sections divider, listing the sections of the deck with a visual marker showing the current one. Uber S5/S8/S12: three flag-shaped bullets, current in green, others black.

### E. Chart decorations (overlays on any chart)

**24. Chart caption & unit label** — text block above or adjacent to the chart describing what is being shown and the units ("Total capital raised through IPOs (tech & non-tech), in USD bn"; "Venture capital historically shows attractive long-term returns"). Typically above the chart, below the slide title.

**25. Axis & data labels** — axis titles, category/tick labels, value labels printed on or next to bars/points. Includes sample-size indicators like McKinsey S8's "(7)(9)(10)(32)..." ellipses under the x-axis.

**26. Legend** — color-swatch-plus-category-name bar. Usually at top or bottom of the chart. Can also label a grid of icons (Harvey-balls legend with 5 fill states) or a map (two color swatches for states).

**27. Annotation callout** — overlay on or near a chart carrying emphasis. Covers:
- Multiplier / delta label with bracket or arrow ("11.6x", "+340", "+154%", "x2", "8x")
- Floating stat label with curved arrow ("$300-400 billion estimated future value", "10X fewer accidents with AV cars", "166% increase", "$1.6 billion estimated project value")
- Dark-rounded-rectangle banner over the chart ("AV safety has markedly improved in recent years", "Costs", "Only a $3,000 difference")
- Brace with label ("Weighted average: -5%")
- Methodology box above or alert callout below a chart (McKinsey S9)
- Illustrative/exemplary tag ("EXAMPLARY" label on McKinsey S3)
- Operator glyph connecting two charts (× between panels on McKinsey S7; → in decompositions)

All of these are variants of "text/graphic overlay that points at a chart element." For grading purposes they fire the same pattern checks (does it have a takeaway? is it readable?).

**28. Tab / filter bar** — pill-style selector row above a chart, usually highlighting the "active" selection. Looks like dashboard navigation. McKinsey S5-S10 all have "Tech IPOs | All IPOs" and "Capital raised | Market cap" tab pairs. This is a strong tell that the slide was extracted from a larger interactive dashboard.

### F. Decorations

**29. Icon** — small symbolic glyph. Variants: semantic (handshake for partnership, gavel for regulation, coin stack for savings, flag for country), decorative (shapes, dots), or wayfinding (chevrons, arrows between panels). Single atom; grading pattern is "is the icon carrying semantic weight or is it filler?"

## What emerged that surprised me

1. **"Annotation callout" is one atom, not five.** I initially wanted separate atoms for multiplier labels, floating stat bubbles, rounded-rect banners, operator glyphs, methodology boxes. But they all do the same job (overlay text/graphic pointing at a chart element) and grading patterns treat them identically.

2. **"Sidebar" shows up on nearly every analytical slide in both decks.** The right-column Key findings / Key Implications / THE REAL STORY panel is almost a universal convention for modern consulting decks. Worth promoting to a first-class atom rather than treating as incidental body text.

3. **"Kicker" is really common and often AI-generated.** Both decks use it (McKinsey as section numbers, Uber implicitly via section menu). Student decks often add LLM-style kickers ("So What?", "Key Implications", "THE OPPORTUNITY | 1 OF 3") that are fine on one slide and a template-saturation tell when they recur on every slide. Separating kicker from title lets grading patterns count kicker-recurrence independently.

4. **Chart primitives collapse to 5 types.** Bar, line/area, waterfall, scatter/bubble, map. Every chart on 31 slides is one of these (plus Harvey-balls which is really a matrix). Keeps the chart taxonomy small.

5. **Tab/filter bar is a dashboard tell.** It shows up on McKinsey slides 5-10 and signals "this was extracted from a PowerBI/Tableau-style dashboard and pasted into a deck." Worth flagging separately because a POV deck with static tab navigation is doing something semantically confused (tabs imply interactivity; PDF doesn't have it).

6. **Section menu is distinct from process arrow.** They look similar (list of items with current marker / arrow-connected steps) but they do different jobs: section menu is structural (deck navigation), process arrow is content (sequence of steps). Keep separate.

## How this maps to grading patterns

Each atom suggests specific pattern checks:

| Atom | Example grading patterns |
|---|---|
| Title (1) | Action-title-vs-topic-label classifier; Golden-Rule check (does body support title?); recurrence of kicker in title |
| Kicker (2) | Count of kickers across deck → AI-scaffolding saturation signal |
| Source line (4) | Source-per-quantitative-claim Gate; source-specificity check ("Web research" vs "Capital IQ") |
| Footnote (5) | Footnote-but-no-citation fallback; chart-footnote-for-methodology check |
| Stat block (12) | Stat-has-unit check; stat-has-source check |
| Chart primitives (13-17) | Chart-has-caption check; axis-labeled check; legend-if-multiseries check |
| Sidebar (11) | Sidebar-length check (bullets vs paragraphs); sidebar-parallelism-across-slides check |
| N-card grid (21) | Card-parallelism check (three cards with identical sub-labels = template saturation); card-body-length variance |
| Data table (22) | Table-arithmetic-ties check; header-row-consistency |
| Process arrow (19) | Step-count vs narrative consistency |
| Comparison panel (20) | Parallel-structure check (are both columns structurally parallel?) |
| Annotation callout (27) | Chart-has-takeaway check; annotation-color-consistency; over-hedging in annotation language |
| Tab/filter bar (28) | Dashboard-residue gate: static deck with tabs that don't do anything |
| Icon (29) | Semantic-vs-decorative check; icon-consistency across slides |

Patterns that don't map to any single atom (e.g., "deck feels LLM-scaffolded overall") operate at the slide composition level, not the atom level.

## Next step

Use this taxonomy as the *output schema* for the Tier 2 parser (pymupdf + Claude vision) we discussed. Every chunk in the markdown output gets one of the 29 atom labels. Patterns then run against the labeled chunks deterministically. Example output shape:

```
<element type="kicker">EXECUTIVE SUMMARY</element>
<element type="title" action="true">Behind the $10B milestone, the Americas core is stalling...</element>
<element type="title-divider"/>
<element type="n-card-grid" count="4">
  <card index="1" icon="numeric-01" heading="Americas growth is an accounting artifact">...</card>
  ...
</element>
<element type="sidebar" style="dark-panel" placement="bottom-banner">WHAT I'M PROPOSING Three 4-10-week diagnostic workstreams...</element>
<element type="source">Source: Lululemon Athletica Inc., Form 10-K (FY2024)...</element>
<element type="brand-mark">Lululemon Intelligence Brief | G. Gould</element>
<element type="page-number">1</element>
```

Two advantages over ADE's typing scheme:

- **Semantic labels** (`kicker`, `sidebar`, `stat-block`) instead of just surface types (`text`, `figure`, `marginalia`). Patterns can target the right things.
- **Composition-aware** (N-card grid is a parent with card children; table has row children). Makes it possible to check parallelism across cards/rows without reconstructing structure from bboxes.

## Stress test (v1 → v2)

Tested v1 taxonomy against three additional decks, totaling 38 more slides:

1. **halle-benson.pdf** (student, 10pp, URBN conversation deck — clean modern design)
2. **braden-park-fisher.pdf** (student, 13pp, Nike performance pivot — editorial style with hero photo)
3. **bcg-loose-dogs-dallas.pdf** (BCG reference, 15 of 57 pp sampled — classic BCG house style)

### What held up

- **Kicker** appeared on every halle-benson body slide ("EXECUTIVE SUMMARY", "WHAT I NOTICED", "THE OPPORTUNITY", "PROPOSED NEXT STEPS", "APPENDIX"). Confirms kicker as a high-value atom worth separating from title.
- **Sidebar panel** showed up on nearly every analytical slide in all three decks, including a new BCG variant (green pill/banner at bottom of slide acting as horizontal takeaway banner). Sidebar is truly universal.
- **Stat block** as a standalone content primitive showed up repeatedly — halle-benson runs stat rows of 4-6 oversized figures below nearly every body slide, braden-park-fisher uses vertical stat stacks beside charts with colored top-accent bars. Same atom, different styling.
- **N-card grid** handled every variant: 4-card (halle S1), 3-card with colored accent bars (halle S4, S6, S7), 3-card with numbered circles and no icons (braden S9), 3-card with icon + pill footer (BCG S15), vertical-stacked variant (Uber S19, braden S10).
- **Annotation callout** absorbed every new form seen: circled values on tables (BCG S11, S14), curly-brace groupers spanning bar segments (BCG S13), CAGR-pointing arrows with badge (BCG S12), A/B letter markers on bullets that mirror chart markers (BCG S10), colored-pill footers under cards (BCG S15). All same atom.
- **Data table** covered BCG's methodology table (S4) and halle-benson's appendix metrics table (S9).
- **Bar / line / waterfall chart primitives** covered every chart I saw, including braden's waterfall (S8), halle's horizontal bars (S2, S3, S5), BCG's stacked bars with CAGR overlays (S12, S13).

### What broke (real gaps)

**Gap 1: Photo / image atom missing.** Both braden-park-fisher (S1 hero Nike shoe photo) and BCG (S12 photo of DAS bite report box with physical form overlay) use photos as content elements. These are not icons (too big, not glyphic), not decorative motifs (semantically meaningful), and not charts. Need a dedicated atom.

**New atom 30: Image / photo** — photographic or illustrative image used as a content or brand element. Variants: hero photo (title slide), product shot, scene photo, mood image, decorative abstract graphic (McKinsey title rays). Grading hooks: is the image relevant to the point being made? Does it earn its space on the slide?

**Gap 2: Atom 19 scope too narrow.** BCG slide 5 ("Dallas dogs can be conceptualized as buckets and flows") is a custom illustrated system diagram — faucets pouring water into buckets with cracks, every element numbered and captioned. Braden-park-fisher slide 5 is a six-phase funnel with labeled phases and descriptions. Neither fits "process arrow / timeline" cleanly.

**Atom 19 renamed and broadened: "Process / flow diagram"** — any sequenced or system-level visual that is not a standard chart type. Covers:
- Process arrow / timeline (horizontal numbered steps with arrow connectors)
- Section menu (mini-TOC used as divider)
- Funnel (stacked rectangles of decreasing size showing progression)
- Custom conceptual diagram (BCG's bucket-and-flows illustration; any metaphor-driven system visual)
- Arrow-header row (phase arrows above a table, like Uber S17)
- Swimlane / Gantt visual
- Decomposition with operator glyphs (× or → connecting charts)

This replaces the old atoms 19 (Process arrow / timeline) and 23 (Section menu) — section menu becomes a variant of the broader atom. Taxonomy shrinks by one net (atom 23 removed, atom 30 added).

### What I almost added but didn't

- **Filename stamp** (BCG has "20160826_1130_Council_Deck.pptx" in the bottom-left corner of every slide). It's a file identifier, different from source or brand mark. But only BCG does this, and it's essentially a brand mark variant (recurring corner element). Folding into atom 7 (brand mark).
- **Cross-reference numbered markers** (BCG uses green circled numbers 1-7 to tie bucket-diagram elements to descriptive text; braden uses numbered circles to link exec-summary steps). This is not an atom — it's two atoms (annotation callout + content element) used together as a pattern. Don't proliferate.
- **Color-coded highlighted bar** (BCG S13 peer chart has Dallas bar in orange while peers are green). Just bar chart styling. No new atom.
- **Multi-level column header** (BCG S15 groups 3 columns under "Field work" and "Shelter work" super-headers). Captured within N-card grid / data table structure.
- **Horizontal takeaway banner** (BCG S10 bottom banner; halle S1 bottom-banner "WHAT I'M PROPOSING"; Uber S3 teal banner). These fire the same grading patterns as sidebars (they carry the slide's takeaway). Decision: annotation callout handles the overlay-on-chart case; paragraph or sidebar handles the banner case. Don't add a separate "takeaway banner" atom — just note that banner/sidebar/annotation can all carry takeaways, and grading pattern is "does every analytical slide have exactly one takeaway and is it a committal statement?"

### What this tells us about the taxonomy

- **Core atoms are stable.** Frame + text + core chart primitives + N-card grid + sidebar show up on essentially every deck. Grading patterns built on these will fire on almost every slide.
- **Specialty atoms are worth keeping even at low frequency.** Harvey-balls, tab bar, and scatter/bubble each fire specific grading patterns (Harvey-balls legend check, tab-bar dashboard-residue flag, bubble-axis-direction-convention check). Absence just means "slide doesn't trigger that check," not "atom is wrong."
- **Annotation callout is the most versatile atom.** It absorbed at least 8 distinct visual forms across the 5 decks. Any parser needs to be generous about what counts as one.
- **Process / flow diagram is the most abstract atom.** Covers 6+ visual patterns (section menu, timeline, funnel, swimlane, conceptual diagram, arrow header). A parser will need a vision model to classify what's inside this atom category; text extraction alone won't cut it.

Taxonomy is ready to use as a parser output schema.

## Second stress test (v2 unchanged)

Tested v2 taxonomy against three additional student decks, 34 more slides:

1. **andres-alberto-arroyo-flores.pdf** (11pp, Apple agentic AI — orange/black, heavy use of banner titles)
2. **carter-johansen.pdf** (12pp, Robinhood — neon-green accents, stat-heavy exec summary)
3. **larson-mckay-brown.pdf** (11pp, Adobe — full-bleed red title/closing slides, includes "Thank you" close)

### Result: no new atoms needed

All 34 slides decomposed cleanly into the v2 atom list. The taxonomy held without modification.

### Styling variants observed (not new atoms)

These all appeared across the three decks and confirm the atoms are resilient to styling variance:

- **Title-in-banner** (andres has title in full-width dark banner; larson has title on full-bleed red). Styling of atom 1 (title), not a new atom.
- **Kicker fused into title line** ("Executive Summary: [action title]" on larson S2; "Appendix A: [title]" on carter). Still atom 1 + atom 2, visually joined.
- **Title with left-edge accent bar** (carter uses small green accent bar to the left of every title). Styling of atom 1.
- **Sidebar with colored header bar** (carter's green/dark PFOF/NII panels; larson's red "Proposal" panel). Styling of atom 11 (sidebar).
- **Cards with numbered circle badges** (01, 02, 03 circles appear in 6 of the 7 decks tested). Captured within atom 21 (N-card grid); circles are card sub-elements.
- **Cards with colored pill-badge labels** (larson's year timestamps "2023-2024", "2025", "2026 onward" at top of each card; andres's orange KPI pill footers). Styling of N-card grid.
- **Color-coded table cells/rows** (carter's color-coded "Primary Account Position" row with red SUPPLEMENTAL / green CORE / yellow GROWING; carter's red-coded negative rate values). Within atom 22 (data table) styling.
- **Numbered bibliography with circle badges** (carter's Appendix A with green circle markers). Styling of atom 9 (bulleted list).
- **Bottom takeaway banner** (carter's black "HARD DEADLINE" banner; andres's black bottom banner; larson's red "THE ASK" banner). All captured by atom 27 (annotation callout) operating slide-wide rather than chart-wide.
- **Pull quote with attribution** (carter S4 has "Enhanced execution quality reporting..." — SEC Final Rules, Nov 2024 in a bordered quote block). Single instance across all 7 decks tested. Variant of atom 9 (paragraph) styled as a quote. Not adding a dedicated atom on one sighting.
- **Closing "Thank you" slide** (larson S9). Compositionally identical to a title slide: atom 1 (title) + atom 8 (metadata block) + atom 7 (brand mark). No new atom.

### Atom added: Pull quote / testimonial

The pull quote pattern has now appeared on **6+ slides across 3 decks**:

- **Bain S11** — two "Luxury consumer" quotes

Six sightings across three decks by three different firms = stable pattern. Promoting to first-class atom.

**Atom 30 (new): Pull quote / testimonial** — a short verbatim quotation from an external speaker (industry expert, customer, survey respondent, analyst, client), rendered in italics, followed by a named-role attribution. Often decorated with a quotation-mark glyph or speech-bubble icon. Functions as supporting evidence *distinct from the deck writer's own prose*.

**Distinct from:**
- Paragraph (atom 9): pull quote has explicit external attribution; paragraph is deck-author prose
- Annotation callout (atom 27): annotation points to a chart element; pull quote is standalone evidence
- Sidebar (atom 11): a pull quote can live inside a sidebar, but it's an atomic content type — a sidebar can contain bullets, stats, or a pull quote, and the three fire different grading patterns

**Grading hooks:**
- Does the quote have a verifiable source (named role, named organization)?
- Is the quote load-bearing for the slide's argument, or decorative?
- Is the quote length appropriate (2 sentences max) for a consulting deck?
- Are multiple quotes parallel in attribution format across the deck?
