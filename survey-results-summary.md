## Key Themes from Open-Ended Feedback

### 1. Students want more case interview practice in class (~10 mentions)

Students repeatedly ask for live case walkthroughs, in-class practice, examples of how to structure problems, and watching TAs or the professor run a case. Several want case prep front-loaded earlier in the semester.

> "I honestly would love to learn more hard skills and practice those in class (such as how to case and examples of hard skills used in casing) to at least give me an idea of what I could practice outside of the class."

> "I would prefer in class to almost do practice cases. Like run through them as a class and brainstorm together to see other people's insights. Possibly calling down one or two students to do a case in front of everyone. Or seeing a TA go through one with Professor Murff."

> "I would love to see more of examples in class of interviewing and how to structure problems not just teaching the principles."

> "I would like to have some more focus on Case prep and strategies early on in the class to prepare us better for case practice."

### 2. Reading quizzes feel low-value (~6 mentions)

Multiple students say the readings aren't helpful and the quizzes don't add value. This aligns with the quantitative ratings (19% "not valuable," 50% "somewhat valuable").

> "I think the reading quizzes aren't super helpful. I also would like to dive more into casing."

> "I feel like the readings haven't been super helpful."

### 3. Assignment organization and deadlines need clarity (~7 mentions)

Confusion between the course website and Learning Suite is a recurring pain point. Students want one place for all assignments, clearer deadline reminders, and better alignment between the schedule on the website and Learning Suite.

> "Having the website and learning suite is confusing, we should be just going to one place for grading/info/assignments/content."

> "The organization of assignments is very poorly managed/updated. Emails need to have more info about what assignments are due and when, not just the day before they are due."

> "It would be nice if deadlines and assignments were much more clear as the semester went on. They were clear when we reviewed the syllabus, but it would go a long way to hear clearer reminders about deadlines."

> "I only wish that the assignments and projects due were on learning suite. It's hard to flip back and forth with the days sometimes not matching up."

### 4. AI demos need recorded tutorials (~5 mentions)

Students want video recordings of Antigravity/VSCode setup and demos they can follow at their own pace. In-class demos move too fast for some to follow while doing it simultaneously on their own machines.

> "It would be helpful if there was screen recordings on zoom of the professor doing certain things on VS code or antigravity. Such as installing the software, running prompts, managing terminator. I feel like having pre-class demos for those sort of things would improve the flow in class."

> "I think it could be useful to instead record videos of them so people can follow along on their time. If I look down to my computer I miss certain buttons you click. Then if I just watch then I don't fully remember afterwards."

> "It would be great if there were videos created of setting up the AI bots. Specifically installing it setting it up on your computer, downloading skills, giving it context getting it to create files that can be exported... It has been pretty difficult doing the actual set up of the AI and I'm not sure that I have done it right even now!"

### 5. Lectures sometimes lack structure (~5 mentions)

Some students feel class sessions bounce around, lack clear learning objectives, or get off-topic. They want more structured, focused lectures with explicit takeaways.

> "I think class sometimes is a bit boring, there's not too much structure and we do a bit of everything."

> "I think it would be helpful to have a more clear learning objective for each lecture."

> "Sometimes its hard to follow whats going on in class because we bounce around so much."

> "The actual class lectures get off topic very often... I want the in-class experience to be improved through either more structured lectures, or bringing the outside of class assignments to class more."

### 6. Real-world examples and consultant "day-in-the-life" (~4 mentions)

Students want to see what consultants actually do---real case studies, real deliverables, and a clearer picture of the daily work.

> "Understanding what consultants actually do like case studies or examples of what they have done."

> "I think something that could be improved is helping us to understand a day in the life of a consultant and practicing all of those things."

> "Sometimes the little frameworks we're given seem a little useless. (like The 7-Step Problem-Solving Process, maybe its because I'm not sure if these are actually used in real life, if they are maybe it'd be helpful to see how real consultants actually use them)"

### 7. P1 could use more guidance (~3 mentions)

Students want more direction on research methods beyond AI, examples of strong deliverables from other students, and possibly weekly milestones instead of a single deadline.

> "I'd maybe like to learn more about how to use outside sources to support our case. 10-k/financial data is good, but what else could I do?"

> "P1 is great. I wish we had a checklist / guidance on how to research beyond AI capabilities."

> "I feel that there could be more structure with the client deliverables. For example, rather than us having P2 due in a month, maybe every week one slide is due... It would be great to also have examples from other students/real consultants."

### 8. TA engagement and grading timeliness (~3 mentions)

A few students note slow grading and want TAs to be more consistently available and engaged.

> "It is important for TA's to be fully engaged. It seems like a few have many other prior commitments and are very late with grading, casing etc."

> "The TA's have been slightly slow in grading what we have done."

### 9. Antigravity vs. Claude/other tools (~3 mentions)

A small group of students prefer using Claude, Cursor, or Ghostty directly and find Antigravity harder to use or less capable. Consider positioning Antigravity as one option alongside direct Claude/VSCode use.

> "Antigravity is good but claude is way better at everything we've been doing, even just the free version. Antigravity is easy to get lost with and isn't extremely capable all the time."

> "I don't use antigravity. I've been using claude and codex in VSCode and Ghostty."

> "Sometimes the heavy focus on AI takes away from being taught how to think like a consultant."

---

## Highest-Priority Action Items

1. **Reduce or rethink reading quizzes.** The data is unambiguous---this is the least valued assessment. Consider replacing with a lighter-touch engagement check or folding key readings into class discussion.

2. **Add more case practice to class sessions.** This is the #1 student request. Even 15--20 minutes of live case walkthroughs or structured partner practice during class would address this.

3. **Consolidate assignment communication.** Align the course website schedule with Learning Suite, or choose one system as the single source of truth for deadlines and submissions.

4. **Create recorded AI/Antigravity setup tutorials.** Short screen-recorded walkthroughs students can watch at their own pace would reduce friction for the ~20% struggling with the tooling.

5. **Sharpen lecture structure.** Post a brief learning objective at the start of each class and tighten transitions between topics to reduce the "bouncing around" feeling.
